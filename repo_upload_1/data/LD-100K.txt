LD-100K: A One-Hundred-Thousand-Character Long-Form Article for Long-Document Language Understanding

This article is a purpose-built, single-document corpus compiled as the test bed for the study reported in "Enhancing TinyBERT to Improve Efficiency and Accuracy". It is written as a continuous work of long-form non-fiction on the humanities of the city: how streets are measured, how archives remember, how migrants make a neighbourhood, how dialects survive a standardisation campaign, and how a heritage site is finally explained to a visitor. The text is deliberately single-document and deliberately long, so that a system has to carry topic, terminology and argument across a hundred thousand characters rather than across a short paragraph. Twelve chapters supply twelve gold topic labels, one for every body paragraph, which makes paragraph-level topic classification and masked-token recovery fully annotated without manual effort.
